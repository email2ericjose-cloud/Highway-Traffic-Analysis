ABSTRACT 
Road traffic safety assessment increasingly needs vehicle -level information on how vehicles 
move and interact. Conventional crash records, manual surveys and fixed sensors are 
retrospective, costly or limited in coverage. Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) pr ovide a 
flexible bird’s-eye view from which vehicle trajectories can be extracted over a road section. 
This study proposes an improved YOLO-based vehicle detection and trajectory analysis 
framework for traffic safety monitoring, crash risk assessment and accident hotspot 
identification on mid-block highway sections in Thiruvananthapuram. 
A review of 17 studies shows that UAV trajectories can support time-to-collision (TTC), post-
encroachment time (PET) and extreme -value-based crash risk estimation, but that small 
vehicles, camera motion, mixed traffic and georeferencing  remain open problems, especially 
for Indian traffic with five vehicle classes: two -wheelers, three -wheelers, cars, light 
commercial vehicles and heavy commercial vehicles. The methodology covers UAV flight 
planning with DGPS ground control, CVAT annotatio n, baseline and small-object-optimised 
YOLO detectors, multi -object tracking, DGPS georeferencing, vehicle count, speed and 
density, TTC and PET, and a crash prediction model with hotspot and level-of-service analysis. 
The governing equations are presented. 
The work done so far is limited to a sample UAV video . A detection and tracking pipeline 
built on a pretrained YOLOv8 model and ByteTrack produced 19,133 tracking records for 614 
vehicles in pixel coordinates, whereas a second pretrained  model failed to identify 
motorcycles, showing that existing models are not reliable for local traffic. Trajectories in real-
world coordinates have not been found, because ground coordinates are not yet available. The 
UAV (DJI Neo 2) has not been purchased and the mid-block sections are yet to be selected, so 
field data collection with DGPS control is pending. 
Keywords: UAV, Vehicle Detection, YOLO, Small-Object Detection, Multi-Object Tracking, 
ByteTrack, Vehicle Trajectory, DGPS Georeferencing, Time-to-Collision, Post-Encroachment 
Time, Surrogate Safety Measures, Extreme Value Theory, Highway Traffic Safety

CHAPTER-1 
INTRODUCTION 
1.1 GENERAL 
Road traffic safety is a major concern at highway junctions and mid -block sections, where 
congestion, speed differences, lane changing and unsafe driving contribute to accident risk. 
Safety studies have traditionally depended on crash records, manual obser vations, roadside 
sensors and fixed cameras. Crash records are retrospective and sparse, while manual surveys 
and fixed sensors are costly and observe only the stretch of road within their range. For 
proactive safety analysis, the ability to reconstruct how every vehicle moves before a conflict 
occurs is particularly valuable. 
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) offer a low-cost and flexible platform for capturing high-
resolution traffic imagery over a wide road stretch. From the bird’s-eye view of a UAV, several 
lanes and many interacting vehicles appear in the same frame, so that vehicle trajectories can 
be extracted rather than only point measurements such as flow or speed at a detector location. 
Trajectories of this kind can be converted into traffic parameters such as vehicle count, speed 
and density, and into surrogate safety m easures such as time -to-collision (TTC) and post -
encroachment time (PET). Because conflicts can be evaluated before a crash occurs, surrogate 
safety measures reduce the dependence on long crash histories (Arun et al., 2021). 
Deep learning object detectors such as YOLO, combined with multi-object tracking, now make 
it possible to process long UAV videos automatically. Vehicles seen from a UAV, however, 
occupy only a few pixels, and two-wheelers are the smallest of them. This is a serious difficulty 
for Indian highways, where two -wheelers, three-wheelers, passenger cars, light commercial 
vehicles and heavy vehicles share the carriageway without strict lane discipline. A detector 
that is reliable on benchmark data may therefore not be reliable on local traffic. 
This study proposes an improved YOLO -based detection and trajectory analysis framework 
for traffic safety monitoring, crash risk assessment and accident hotspot identification on mid-
block highway sections in Thiruvananthapuram. Vehicles of five classes (two-wheeler, three-
wheeler, car, light commercial vehicle and heavy commercial vehicle) are to be detected and 
tracked in UAV video, their trajectories georeferenced using DGPS ground control, and the

resulting trajectories used to derive traffic parameters , surrogate safety measures and a crash 
prediction model. This interim report presents the literature review, objectives, data 
requirements, methodology and the part results obtained so far from a sample UAV video. 
1.2 NEED FOR THE STUDY 
The increasing volume and diversity of traffic on urban highways has created a need for safety 
assessment that does not wait for crashes to accumulate. In the absence of detailed vehicle -
level data: 
• Manual surveys and fixed roadside sensors remain costly and limited in spatial 
coverage 
• Interactions between neighbouring vehicles, such as lane changes and sudden braking, 
cannot be observed 
• Detectors trained on international UAV benchmarks may fail on small two -wheelers 
and three-wheelers in local traffic 
• Safety measures cannot be computed reliably because pixel movement has no physical 
scale without georeferencing 
In Thiruvananthapuram there is no locally annotated, DGPS -georeferenced UAV vehicle 
dataset covering the five vehicle classes. The need for such a dataset, and for a detector tuned 
to it, was confirmed by the sample -video tests described in Chapter 6, in w hich pretrained 
models proved unreliable for two-wheelers. 
1.3 PROBLEM STATEMENT 
Existing UAV-based traffic studies rely mainly on generic detectors, optical-flow or clustering 
methods, and rarely pair a small -object-optimised detector with trajectory -level crash risk 
analysis. The challenge lies in detecting all five vehicle classes r eliably from high -altitude 
UAV imagery, converting the detections into georeferenced trajectories, and using those 
trajectories to estimate surrogate safety measures and crash risk on mid-block highway sections 
under heterogeneous Indian traffic.

CHAPTER-2 
LITERATURE REVIEW 
2.1 REVIEW OF LITERATURE 
1. Ke et al. (2017) 
Ke et al. (2017) proposed a framework that combines a KLT tracker with k -means clustering 
to separate traffic streams from moving -camera UAV video. From the separated streams the 
method estimates speed, density and volume of each traffic stream in real time.  The authors 
reported accuracies of 96% for average speed and 87% for vehicle count. 
The study represents an early stage in the development of UAV traffic analysis: it showed that 
aerial video can supply traffic-flow parameters and not only visual records. It is relevant to the 
present work because the count, speed and density estimates pl anned in this study follow the 
same logic, and because comparison with reference counts and speeds is used for validation. 
2. Khan et al. (2017) 
Khan et al. (2017) reviewed the literature on UAV -based traffic studies and proposed a 
universal guiding framework of seven components: scope definition, flight planning, flight 
implementation, data acquisition, data processing and analysis, data interpret ation, and 
optimised traffic application. The authors treated a UAV study as a complete data-acquisition 
system and not merely as video recording. 
They emphasised that flight altitude, camera orientation, weather, lighting and battery limits 
all affect the quality of the final trajectories. For the present study this means that flight 
planning has to be designed around trajectory accuracy and not only around visual coverage. 
3. Xu, Y. et al. (2017) 
Xu et al. (2017) extended the Faster R -CNN framework to detect cars in low -altitude UAV 
imagery at signalised intersections. The study demonstrated robustness to changes in 
illumination and to in -plane rotation of vehicles, and observed that detection spee d stayed 
nearly constant regardless of the number of vehicles in a frame. 
Faster R-CNN is a two -stage detector in which a Region Proposal Network first generates 
candidate regions that a second stage classifies and localises. It can give strong localisation, 

=== PAGE 14 ===
5 
but its computational cost is a concern for long UAV videos. In the present study it serves as 
a two-stage benchmark against which the proposed YOLO detectors are compared. 
4. Du et al. (2018) 
Du et al. (2018) introduced UAVDT, a vehicle-focused aerial benchmark for object detection 
and tracking. The benchmark highlights high object density, small objects, camera motion and 
real-time constraints as the central challenges of UAV vision. 
The study showed that generic object detectors can lose performance under UAV -specific 
conditions. UAVDT is valuable for pretraining and benchmarking, but it cannot substitute for 
a locally annotated dataset, which is the reason a site -specific five-class dataset is planned in 
this study. 
5. Liu et al. (2020) 
Liu et al. (2020) proposed UAV -YOLO, which modifies the YOLOv3 Darknet backbone to 
enlarge the receptive field and enrich spatial information for small objects seen from a UAV. 
The authors also built a dedicated UAV -viewed training dataset and a training m ethod suited 
to it. 
This is one of the foundational works on small-object detection tailored to the UAV viewpoint. 
It supports the view that the detector architecture should be adapted to the scale distribution of 
UAV imagery and informs the small-object head proposed in this study. 
6. Outay et al. (2020) 
Outay et al. (2020) reviewed the applications of UAVs in road safety, traffic monitoring and 
highway infrastructure management. The review discussed computer vision methods for traffic 
flow analysis, risk assessment and damage assessment, and identified the barriers to wide-scale 
UAV deployment, such as regulations, flight limitations and data processing demands, together 
with countermeasures. 
The review provides the overall context for the present study and confirms the analytical 
opportunities of UAV data as well as the practical constraints, such as permissions, that must 
be cleared before field acquisition. 

=== PAGE 15 ===
6 
7. Arun et al. (2021) 
Arun et al. (2021) carried out a systematic mapping review of 549 studies on surrogate safety 
assessment using traffic conflict techniques and developed a broad framework for it. They 
stressed the need for clearer relationships between conflicts and crashes an d for a better 
understanding of how individual measures should be used. 
The review shows that conflicts can be evaluated before a crash occurs, reducing dependence 
on long crash histories. It also cautions that surrogate measures identify conflict conditions and 
not confirmed crashes, so any crash model developed in this study  is presented as estimated 
risk. 
8. Aharon et al. (2022) 
Aharon et al. (2022) proposed BoT -SORT, a tracker that combines motion and appearance 
information for data association and includes camera -motion compensation. Camera -motion 
compensation is important for UAV video, where even a small camera movement shifts all 
vehicles in the image. 
A tracker that assumes a stationary camera may confuse camera motion with vehicle 
movement. BoT-SORT is therefore one of the trackers to be compared on identity continuity 
in this study. 
9. Zhang et al. (2022) 
Zhang et al. (2022) proposed ByteTrack, which associates every detection box, including low-
confidence ones, instead of discarding them. This helps to recover partially occluded objects 
whose detector confidence temporarily drops, a situation common for small vehicles seen from 
above. 
ByteTrack is the tracker used in the pipeline test on the sample video reported in Chapter 6, 
where it maintained vehicle identities across frames. 
10. Zong et al. (2022) 
Zong et al. (2022) combined deep -learning-based vehicle tracking with time -to-collision 
analysis on UAV video at road intersections. The study demonstrated UAV -derived 
trajectories as a practical tool for proactive collision risk assessment and highlighted the  
advantage of UAV observation in overcoming the occlusion that affects conventional tracking 
equipment. 

=== PAGE 16 ===
7 
The work is a direct example of the detection–tracking–safety chain that the present study aims 
to build, and it highlights the value of such analysis for urban road agencies and traffic safety 
management. 
11. Wang et al. (2023) 
Wang et al. (2023) proposed UAV -YOLOv8, a modification of YOLOv8 for small -object 
detection in UAV aerial photography scenarios. The work belongs to the recent line of YOLO 
research that focuses on preserving fine spatial information for tiny targets. 
It supports adapting the detector architecture to the scale distribution of UAV imagery instead 
of using a generic model, which is the principle behind the improved detector of this study. 
12. Berghaus et al. (2024) 
Berghaus et al. (2024) built a drone -based trajectory dataset of over 8,000 trajectories on a 
German highway, covering an off-ramp, an on-ramp and congested traffic. The pipeline used 
YOLOv5 detection with three-dimensional camera calibration to project vehicles onto the road 
surface, followed by post-processing of the trajectories. 
The derived speed and acceleration were compared with independent sources, and the data 
were applied to traffic -flow and accident-risk analysis. The study shows that calibration and 
quality assessment must be planned as part of the measurement chain, and t hat a trajectory 
dataset is a research output in its own right. 
13. Xu, L. et al. (2024) 
Xu et al. (2024) improved YOLOv8n with multi -scale feature fusion, conditionally 
parameterised convolutions and the Wise-IoU loss for small-object detection in UAV images. 
The model achieved state-of-the-art accuracy on the VisDrone benchmark for small, partially 
occluded objects. 
It outperformed other lightweight YOLO variants and Faster R-CNN on small-object accuracy 
while remaining lightweight. The multi-scale fusion and Wise-IoU components are among the 
changes to be tested in the improved detector of this study. 
14. Zhao et al. (2024) 
Zhao et al. (2024) proposed RT -DETR, a real -time end -to-end transformer detector that 
removes the non -maximum suppression post -processing step. The RT -DETR-R50 model 

=== PAGE 17 ===
8 
reached 53.1% AP on COCO at 108 frames per second on a T4 GPU, outperforming advanced 
YOLO detectors in both speed and accuracy. 
RT-DETR serves, together with Faster R -CNN, as a comparison detector against which the 
proposed detector is benchmarked. 
15. Fonod et al. (2025) 
Fonod et al. (2025) developed a multi -drone pipeline for extracting georeferenced vehicle 
trajectories, demonstrated over 20 intersections. Vehicle detections were used during image 
registration so that moving vehicles do not dominate the estimation of camera m otion, and 
trajectories were stabilised before being georeferenced through orthophoto and master -frame 
alignment. 
The study demonstrates that trajectory extraction and georeferencing should be considered 
together, and it supports the DGPS georeferencing  step and the frame -stabilisation approach 
planned in this study. 
16. Kumar and Sarkar (2025) 
Kumar and Sarkar (2025) collected UAV-based vehicle trajectory data at the Panipat Toll Plaza 
on NH -44, an Indian highway toll plaza with heterogeneous, lane -changing traffic. Lane -
Changing Time-to-Collision, a surrogate safety measure, was combined with E xtreme Value 
Theory (EVT) to estimate crash risk. 
The authors found a negative relationship between vehicle size and conflict involvement across 
vehicle categories. The study is closely aligned with the present work because it connects UAV 
data, heterogeneous Indian traffic, lane changing and crash-risk estimation, and it shows why 
class-wise analysis is essential. 
17. Maji and Ghosh (2025) 
Maji and Ghosh (2025) extracted trajectories from high-resolution drone video at roundabouts 
under non-lane-based mixed traffic. TTC and maximum Delta-V were used as surrogate safety 
measures, and Extreme Value Theory was applied for conflict analysis, moving beyond simple 
threshold-based conflict counting. 

=== PAGE 18 ===
9 
The study is important for Indian applications because road -user interactions do not always 
follow strict lane-based assumptions, and it supports the use of statistical modelling of extreme 
conflicts in this study. 
2.2 SUMMARY OF LITERATURE 
The reviewed studies show that UAVs are well established as a low-cost, flexible platform for 
road safety, traffic monitoring and highway infrastructure surveys (Outay et al., 2020; Khan et 
al., 2017). Classical tracking and clustering methods (KLT with k -means) and two -stage 
detectors (Faster R-CNN) have both demonstrated accurate traffic parameter and vehicle-count 
estimation from aerial video (Ke et al., 2017; Xu et al., 2017). 
Standard YOLO detectors are fast, but recent studies (UAV -YOLO, UAV -YOLOv8, 
YOLOv8n with multi -scale fusion) show that unmodified YOLO underperforms on small, 
high-altitude vehicle objects, and benchmarks such as UAVDT confirm that generic detectors 
lose accuracy under UAV conditions (Liu et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2024; Du 
et al., 2018). Tracking methods such as ByteTrack and BoT-SORT address identity continuity, 
and BoT-SORT adds camera-motion compensation that is relevant to UAV video (Zhang et 
al., 2022; Aharon et al., 2022). 
UAV-derived vehicle trajectories have been used successfully with surrogate safety measures 
such as time -to-collision to assess crash and conflict risk at intersections, toll plazas and 
roundabouts, including on Indian roads (Zong et al., 2022; Kumar and Sarkar, 2025; Maji and 
Ghosh, 2025). Trajectory datasets and georeferencing  pipelines show that calibration, post -
processing and independent validation are essential (Berghaus et al., 2024; Fonod et al., 2025). 
Very few studies, however, combine an improved YOLO-based detector with trajectory-level 
crash risk and hotspot analysis for Indian mixed-traffic conditions. 
2.3 IDENTIFIED RESEARCH GAPS 
Based on the review of literature, the following research gaps have been identified: 
2.3.1 Generic detectors instead of small-object-optimised YOLO 
Existing UAV traffic studies rely mainly on generic detectors, optical -flow or clustering 
methods rather than modern small-object-optimised YOLO architectures. 

=== PAGE 19 ===
10 
2.3.2 Computational cost of two-stage detectors 
Two-stage detectors such as Faster R -CNN achieve good small -object accuracy but are 
computationally heavy for near real-time, highway-scale processing. 
2.3.3 Lack of evaluation on Indian mixed vehicle classes 
Recent small-object YOLO improvements have not been evaluated on Indian mixed vehicle -
class highway traffic (two-wheeler, three-wheeler, car, LCV, HCV). 
2.3.4 Detection and safety analysis treated separately 
UAV-trajectory-based crash risk and surrogate safety studies exist, but are rarely paired with 
an improved, small-object-optimised detector or extended into hotspot and LOS -based safety 
applications. 
2.3.5 Mid-block sections largely unexplored 
UAV-based, trajectory -level traffic safety analysis for mid -block sections remains largely 
unexplored. 
2.4 RESEARCH CONTRIBUTION 
The present study addresses the identified gaps through the following contributions: 
• Acquisition of UAV imagery and construction of an annotated, DGPS -georeferenced 
vehicle dataset with five vehicle classes 
• A baseline YOLO detector and an improved small -object-optimised YOLO detector, 
benchmarked against Faster R-CNN and RT-DETR on the same local test set 
• Multi-object tracking and georeferenced trajectories from which vehicle count, speed 
and density are derived and validated 
• Surrogate safety measures (TTC and PET) and a crash prediction model with severity 
ranking of potential collisions 
• Accident hotspot identification and level-of-service assessment presented through GIS 
visualisation 
2.5 CONCLUSION OF LITERATURE REVIEW 
The literature establishes that UAV imagery, deep -learning detection, multi -object tracking 
and surrogate safety measures can be combined into a complete workflow from video to crash

risk. It also shows that small and partially occluded vehicles, camera motion, mixed traffic, 
trajectory noise and georeferencing remain open challenges, and that local annotated data are

The present study builds on these methods while addressing the gaps through a locally 
annotated, DGPS -referenced five -class dataset, a small -object-optimised detector and 
trajectory-level safety analysis. Its evaluation covers not only detector accuracy bu t also the 
quality and safety relevance of the resulting trajectories.

The study is set in Thiruvananthapuram city, Kerala, India. Thiruvananthapuram is the capital 
of the state of Kerala and a rapidly urbanising centre with a mix of residential, commercial, 
institutional and administrative land uses. This land -use mix, toget her with major road 
corridors, produces a traffic stream in which two -wheelers, three -wheelers, cars, light 
commercial vehicles and heavy commercial vehicles all share the road. This mixed vehicle 
composition makes the city a suitable setting for testing U AV-based detection and trajectory 
analysis under heterogeneous traffic.

5.1 GENERAL 
The methodology is a UAV -based workflow that moves from field acquisition to detection, 
tracking, georeferenced trajectories and finally to traffic parameters and crash risk. Each step 
uses data from the previous one, so errors in an early stage can propag ate: a missed vehicle 
breaks a trajectory, an identity switch joins two vehicles, and a coordinate error changes the 
relative position of two vehicles and therefore their TTC or PET. Detection, tracking, 
georeferencing and post-processing are therefore treated as one connected system. The sample-
video work reported in Chapter 6 does not change this methodology.

5.6 FRAME EXTRACTION, STABILISATION AND ORTHOMOSAIC 
Every k-th frame is extracted for annotation to avoid near-duplicate images, whereas detection 
and tracking run on the complete video, as in the sample -video test. To stop camera motion 
from being mistaken for vehicle motion, features are matched between c onsecutive frames, a 
homography is estimated with RANSAC and every frame is warped to a reference frame. 
Detected vehicles are masked out of the feature matching so that moving traffic does not bias 
the camera-motion estimate, the approach followed by Fonod et al. (2025). 
Overlapping stills are processed by photogrammetry (structure from motion) into an 
orthomosaic, from which lane lines, road edges and centrelines are digitised as road geometry. 
Blurred or unusable frames are removed before annotation.

dominant class in Indian traffic. In the output crop in Figure 6.2, labels appear onl y on the 
larger vehicles, whereas the small two-wheelers visible in the scene are left without boxes.

6.8 LIMITATIONS OF EXISTING METHODS 
The sample-video tests show that pre -existing methods, applied as they are, are not accurate 
enough for mixed Indian traffic: 
• A second pretrained model could not identify motorcycles and other two-wheelers 
• In the output of the first model (Figure 6.1), the motorcycle detections carry confidence 
scores of about 0.41 –0.52, which are generally lower than those of cars (up to 0.84), 
consistent with the difficulty of detecting small objects 
• The classes observed (cars, vans, trucks, motorcycles and buses) do not follow the five-
class scheme of this study, and three-wheelers are not among them 
• Tracking worked only in the pixel domain; without ground coordinates the results have 
no physical scale 
These observations are visual. Quantitative accuracy will be reported only after the local 
dataset has been annotated. They nevertheless support the need for a small -object-optimised

35 
detector trained on a local five -class dataset, which is the approach proposed in the 
methodology, and agree with the benchmark evidence that generic detectors lose accuracy 
under UAV conditions (Du et al., 2018). 
6.9 TRAJECTORY GENERATION: CURRENT STATUS 
Vehicle detection and tracking in the pixel domain have been completed on the sample video. 
Trajectories in real-world coordinates have not been found: converting the tracked IDs into 
ground coordinates needs DGPS ground control, which is not available because the coordinates 
have not been given. Until the geometric transformation of Section 5.11 is carried out, pixel 
positions give only relative movement and cannot be reported as distance, speed, TTC or PET. 
6.10 DISCUSSION 
The sample-video work shows that the detection and tracking part of the workflow can be run 
end to end: vehicles are detected, given persistent IDs and collected into a trajectory table. It 
also shows where the workflow cannot yet go. The reliability check confirms the main premise 
of the study, that an off-the-shelf detector cannot be trusted for two -wheeler-heavy traffic, so 
the baseline and improved detectors must be trained on local data. The missing step is 
georeferencing, which depends on the purchase of the UAV, the selection of the sections and 
the DGPS survey. These field activities are therefore the critical path of the project, as reflected 
in the schedule in Chapter 7.
